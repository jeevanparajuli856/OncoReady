# 3. Functional Vital Sign — VCOM

This project name provides a strong clinical clue.

## Clinical meaning

In healthcare literature, gait speed and mobility measures are sometimes described as a:

> **functional vital sign**

Examples include:

- walking speed
- Timed Up and Go (TUG)
- sit-to-stand testing
- mobility/frailty assessments

These measures can reveal health decline that traditional measurements such as:

```text
HR
BP
Temperature
SpO2
```

may not capture.

## Likely product

A patient performs a functional test:

```text
Walk test
or
Timed Up and Go
or
Sit-to-stand
```

Then:

```text
Phone camera / wearable / sensor
        ↓
Movement analysis
        ↓
Functional score
        ↓
Compare against baseline
        ↓
Detect decline
        ↓
Notify care team
```

## Cancer use case

Cancer patients can experience:

- chemotherapy fatigue
- neuropathy
- weakness
- functional decline
- frailty
- fall risk

So the product could monitor:

```text
Cancer treatment
       ↓
Functional decline
       ↓
Objective measurement
       ↓
Earlier intervention
```

## Potential AI/technical stack

Likely possibilities:

- computer vision
- pose estimation
- IMU sensors
- gait analytics
- trend analysis
- frailty scoring
- predictive model

## Why this competitor matters

This could be one of the most clinically credible projects because VCOM is a medical school.

Their pitch may emphasize:

```text
objective measurement
rather than
self-reported symptoms
```

## Difference from OncoReady

Functional Vital Sign:

```text
Is the patient's physical function deteriorating?
```

OncoReady:

```text
Is tomorrow's infusion operationally and clinically ready?
```

# Likely Judge Comparison Questions

Prepare specifically for:

### "How are you different from BayouCare?"

Best conceptual distinction:

> BayouCare is designed around the entire cancer journey. OncoReady is designed around a specific operational failure point: whether a scheduled infusion is actually ready to happen. We identify blockers before the treatment day, route clinical and logistical issues to different owners, and track them until closure.

---

### "How are you different from Bayou Care Navigator or Project Compass?"

> They likely focus on helping the patient understand and navigate cancer care. OncoReady focuses on the treatment operation itself — identifying blockers before infusion and ensuring someone owns and resolves each issue.

---

### "How are you different from Functional Vital Sign?"

> Functional monitoring can detect patient decline. OncoReady can consume a clinical concern like that as one readiness signal, but our product coordinates all reasons an infusion could fail — clinical plus logistical — and ensures closure before the appointment.

---

### "Why not put this inside a general cancer app?"

> Because the critical value comes from workflow ownership, deadlines, escalation, and pharmacy/treatment timing — not simply presenting another patient dashboard.

The finalist field likely contains several versions of:

```text

+

+
resource finder
+
caregiver connection
```

There are also more specialized concepts around:


The one confirmed broad technical competitor is **BayouCare**.

The strategic opportunity for OncoReady is therefore **not breadth**.

It is to make one healthcare workflow feel extremely real:

> **A patient has chemotherapy tomorrow. Before pharmacy compounds the drug and before the patient travels to the hospital, OncoReady determines whether the treatment is truly ready, separates clinical from logistical blockers, gives each blocker an owner, and verifies that it is closed.**

That is the clearest wedge currently visible in this finalist field.
