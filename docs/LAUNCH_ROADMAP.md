## 1. Mission

Build the strongest product in the Nexus DevDay final: a polished, technically credible early treatment-continuity platform that completes one memorable rescue journey across one patient, one authorized caregiver, one hospital care team, one transportation pathway, one SMS recipient, and one voice-call recipient.

The presentation should feel like a coherent launch product, not a prototype or collection of disconnected feature proofs. Limitations and environment boundaries belong in this engineering plan and in the presenter's verbal disclosure, not as repeated visual badges that weaken the application experience.

### Winning priorities

| Judging dimension | Target impression | Product proof |
|---|---|---|
| Problem legitimacy | OncoReady intervenes while the treatment can still be preserved | T−7/T−3/T−1 timeline and transportation cutoff |
| Novelty | The product closes a linked oncology treatment chain rather than sending another reminder | Department-owned dependency graph, SLA, patient acknowledgment, and closure evidence |
| Development difficulty | The difficult work is visible and inspectable | Durable event state, real callbacks, transport recovery, FHIR validation, calibrated LightGBM, and SHAP |
| Opportunity of impact | The audience can see how the product changes operations | Lead time, time-to-owner, time-to-closure, unresolved blockers, and final disposition |

### Implementation bar

- Build production-quality interaction and visual polish across every intended screen.
- Core state transitions, deadlines, rejection rules, callbacks, metrics, and model outputs must be generated by working code rather than hard-coded screen swaps.
- Twilio must send and receive a real message on the controlled finals number.
- ElevenLabs must place a real call through Twilio and return a verified callback outcome.
- The custom CareLink Partner Dispatch portal must execute a real persisted request, assignment, failure/recovery, notification, acknowledgment, return-plan, and closure lifecycle for a local transportation vendor.
- Uber Health and Lyft Concierge are post-finals provider adapters. The finals product may show them only in a clearly labeled integration-roadmap panel; it must not imply approved access, connected credentials, live requests, or provider callbacks.
- The FHIR artifact must pass a real validator.
- LightGBM training, calibration, inference, and SHAP explanations must use real pipeline artifacts.
- Every external service must retain a deterministic recovery path so a network failure becomes a credible product state instead of a failed presentation.

### Confirmed human direction

- Follow the must-build product direction in `ONCOREADY_WINNING_STRATEGY.md`.
- Remove the detailed Treatment Readiness Workspace and patient record from the public landing page.
- Rename the public navigation item `Business model` to `Pricing`.
- Publish one centrally editable **Small Infusion Center Pilot** price of **$18,000/year** (`$1,500/month`, billed annually). Larger programs and health systems use custom pricing.
- Add a dedicated access page with branded buttons and no credential prompt:
  - Google button → patient workspace.
  - Microsoft button → caregiver workspace.
  - Apple button → hospital/staff workspace.
  - Email button → transportation coordination workspace.
- Support Twilio SMS, ElevenLabs voice calls, and provider-neutral transportation orchestration across the complete finals journey. The custom CareLink Partner Dispatch portal is the only finals fulfillment path; Uber Health and Lyft Concierge remain future adapters.
- Use a calibrated LightGBM classifier with SHAP explanations.
- Build the ML capability last, after the workflow and event contracts are stable.
- Execute the approved slices through parallel database, backend, frontend, communications, transportation, and ML workstreams after the shared event and API contracts are frozen.

### Winning narrative

OncoReady is not presented as another reminder system, generic no-show predictor, patient portal, or ride-booking button. The differentiating claim is:

> OncoReady detects a recoverable threat days before oncology treatment, gives every dependency an accountable owner and deadline, executes communication and transportation recovery, and proves the continuity plan closed.

Every implemented feature must reinforce that single story. Extra dashboards, generic chatbot behavior, unrelated oncology education, and decorative integrations are out of scope if they do not strengthen early detection, accountable rescue, or verified closure.

#### Market analysis and rationale

- Findhelp publicly lists general annual subscriptions from $5,400 to $75,000+, but explicitly uses custom pricing for healthcare providers. Its public figures are directional context rather than a direct oncology-health-system price comparison: <https://company.findhelp.com/pricing/>.
- Luma Health and Roundtrip use sales-led purchasing rather than public self-serve checkout for enterprise patient engagement and healthcare transportation, supporting OncoReady's `Request a pilot` motion: <https://www.lumahealth.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/LumaHealth_Solutions-Overview.pdf>, <https://roundtriphealth.com/faq/>.
- Twilio SMS is usage-priced per segment plus carrier/number fees, and ElevenLabs voice plans scale by included usage with custom enterprise terms. These costs should remain pass-through rather than being hidden inside unlimited platform pricing: <https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/us>, <https://elevenlabs.io/pricing>.
- Transportation fulfillment remains outside the subscription because the custom dispatch portal coordinates a local vendor rather than bundling the ride cost.

The pilot price is an initial market-positioning hypothesis for a small infusion center, not a validated Ochsner or industry price. Larger organizations remain custom-priced until buyer interviews establish deployment, integration, support, and site complexity.

## 5. Minimum Launch Architecture

### Recommended stack

- Keep React + TypeScript + Vite for the frontend.
- Deploy the existing React/Vite frontend and TypeScript Vercel Node.js Functions as one Vercel project for workflow commands, projections, vendor adapters/webhooks, FHIR generation, and exported LightGBM inference.
- Add Supabase PostgreSQL as persistence only; real identity/authentication remains outside the finals build.
- Use Git-tracked SQL migrations under `supabase/migrations`.
- Use an append-only workflow event table with current-state projections and an outbox/idempotency table.
- Use one scheduler/worker mechanism for T−7/T−3/T−1 outreach and SLA deadlines.
- Do not add Redis, Kafka, microservices, or a general-purpose queue unless measured behavior later proves they are required.

```text
React/Vite role workspaces
        │
        ▼
Generated OpenAPI client
        │
        ▼
TypeScript Vercel Node.js Functions
        ├── deterministic timeline, routing, SLA, and transport policies
        ├── authenticated Cron tick + bounded scheduled/outbox drain
        ├── Twilio messaging adapter + signed callbacks
        ├── ElevenLabs/Twilio voice adapter + signed callbacks
        ├── custom Partner Dispatch portal + normalized transport events
        ├── disabled future-adapter registry for Uber Health and Lyft Concierge
        ├── metrics and FHIR projection
        └── calibrated exported LightGBM inference adapter (final slice)
        │
        ▼
PostgreSQL append-only events + projections + outbox/idempotency
```

### Finals-environment controls

Because the branded access page is not real authentication, the controlled finals deployment requires:

- fixture records only, with no real patient data;
- restrictive CORS/origin policy;
- request size and rate limits;
- one server-managed finals instance or non-guessable scenario token;
- reset/reseed controls;
- test-phone allowlist;
- configured `partner_dispatch` enforcement; Uber Health and Lyft Concierge adapters remain disabled and have no finals credentials;
- vendor-action kill switches;
- Vercel Pro or equivalent per-minute Cron availability; Hobby's daily schedule is insufficient for the finals scheduler;
- durable scheduled/outbox rows are committed before delivery; consequential provider delivery completes inside the bounded authenticated tick rather than post-response background work;
- secrets stored server-side and outside Git;
- no arbitrary phone number or ride destination accepted from the public browser.

### FLOW — Durable early-warning and department-owned work

**User outcome:** Maria is surfaced at least 72 hours before treatment; a single response creates separately owned clinical and transportation work with deadlines and closure evidence.

**Minimum domain:**

- treatment event and appointment-relative cutoffs;
- T−7, T−3, and T−1 signal snapshots;
- barriers and immutable patient verbatim text;
- work items, department threads, owners, SLAs, and escalation;
- caregiver grant and allowlisted projection;
- append-only workflow events, provenance, correlation IDs, and idempotency keys;
- outbox and current-state projections.

**Required transitions:**

```text
draft → sent → department received → owner assigned
      → needs information / accepted
      → action recorded → patient informed
      → patient acknowledged → closed

SLA missed → escalated
appointment changed → cutoff and dependent work recomputed/reopened
```

**Acceptance criteria:**

- every signal says what was known and when;
- transport notice cutoff uses business-day arithmetic;
- explicit symptom/ride barriers bypass priority scoring and route immediately;
- clinical and transport threads have different owners, deadlines, and closure rules;
- invalid or out-of-order close attempts are rejected;
- duplicate events are idempotent;
- patient, caregiver, staff, graph, timeline, and metrics projections stay consistent;
- stale scheduler or source data is visible and never produces false reassurance;
- reset/reseed restores the exact finals scenario.

### SMS — Twilio readiness messaging

**User outcome:** Maria receives and answers one minimal-detail readiness SMS using the same workflow as the web experience.

**Interaction:** `1 ready`, `2 need a ride`, `3 call me`, `4 scheduling help`, `9 repeat`.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- the server sends only to the allowlisted test number;
- queued, sent, delivered, undelivered, answered, and opted-out states are stored as events;
- inbound and status webhooks validate Twilio's signature using the official SDK and raw request data;
- retries and out-of-order callbacks are idempotent;
- STOP/HELP and consent state are enforced;
- `2` opens transport work and `3` opens voice/callback work;
- unstructured clinical text is preserved verbatim and routed to a human without an automated clinical reply;
- undelivered SMS leaves the barrier unresolved and activates the approved voice/manual fallback;
- no frontend code receives Twilio credentials.

Twilio documents asynchronous outbound status callbacks, inbound webhooks, Messaging Service opt-out handling, and signed webhook validation: <https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/guides/outbound-message-status-in-status-callbacks>, <https://www.twilio.com/docs/messaging/guides/webhook-request>, <https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/webhooks/webhooks-security>.

### VOICE — ElevenLabs call through Twilio

**User outcome:** A failed SMS or `call me` reply produces one bounded voice call to the same allowlisted test phone.

**Allowed voice behavior:**

- confirm the intended recipient using the approved finals script;
- repeat or slow the appointment time;
- collect `ready`, `ride help`, `scheduling help`, or `human callback`;
- route symptoms, uncertainty, or unsupported requests to a person;
- never diagnose, triage, prescribe, clear treatment, or close a clinical barrier.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- outbound call initiation is server-only and idempotent;
- call attempts, answered/no-answer/failure, structured response, and callback need become workflow events;
- ElevenLabs post-call/failure webhooks validate their HMAC signature;
- only an allowlisted structured outcome can update workflow state;
- no answer, agent error, or provider outage creates manual callback work and remains unresolved;
- transcripts and audio are not stored by default;
- the call begins with the approved disclosure and the test recipient has consented;
- the deterministic replay path remains available if the provider call fails.

ElevenLabs supports Twilio-connected outbound calls and HMAC-authenticated post-call/failure webhooks: <https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/phone-numbers/twilio-integration/native-integration>, <https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/integrations/twilio/outbound-call>, <https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/workflows/post-call-webhooks>.

### RIDE — Custom Transportation Partner Dispatch portal

**User outcome:** A navigator and local transportation coordinator turn Maria's transportation risk into an eligibility-aware outbound and return plan, request and assign one CareLink partner trip through the custom dispatch portal, recover from one provider failure, and keep the blocker open until Maria acknowledges the confirmed plan.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- deterministic checks run before any request: notice cutoff, funding/eligibility path, operating window, arrival window, outbound and return plan, mobility/escort need, service area, and provider availability;
- only the staff or transportation workspace can initiate the configured request;
- request creation uses an idempotency key and a server-side provider adapter;
- the required `partner_dispatch` adapter persists request, offer, acceptance, assignment, notification, acknowledgment, cancellation/failure, backup, and completion events;
- Uber Health and Lyft Concierge have no finals implementation, credentials, network actions, or callbacks; a non-interactive `Planned adapters` panel may explain that the normalized provider boundary supports future onboarding;
- requested, accepted, driver assigned, patient notified, patient acknowledged, arriving, completed, cancelled, declined, provider unavailable, and backup required remain distinct workflow events;
- a cancelled/failed trip reopens the blocker and permits one controlled retry or manual navigator escalation;
- no option or provider outage keeps the treatment event at risk;
- patient acknowledgment is still required for closure;
- the product never autonomously cancels or reschedules treatment;
- Uber Health and Lyft Concierge names or logos never appear in the active trip, timeline, metrics, or provider-status components;
- the visible interaction, CareLink statuses, recovery, and closure behavior must match the custom local-vendor operating flow, while the finals provider stays fixed in server configuration.

The research and provider decision are documented in [`docs/TRANSPORTATION_RESEARCH_AND_FINALS_DECISION.md`](./TRANSPORTATION_RESEARCH_AND_FINALS_DECISION.md). The external-provider adoption gate cannot be completed before finals, so CareLink Partner Dispatch is the final decision for the presentation. Uber Health and Lyft Concierge remain post-finals integration opportunities behind the normalized provider boundary.

### EVIDENCE — Operational metrics and FHIR R4 artifact

**User outcome:** Investors and hospital stakeholders can inspect evidence derived from the same workflow rather than fixture-only dashboard numbers.

**Computed metrics:**

- lead time at first actionable signal;
- time to owner, acceptance, first action, and closure;
- unresolved blockers at T−72/T−24/T−4;
- SLA breaches and contact attempts;
- delivery/answer/acknowledgment status;
- final kept, clinically rescheduled, administratively rescheduled, cancelled, or unknown disposition.

**Acceptance criteria:**

- a metric changes only when its underlying event changes;
- the FHIR R4 bundle generated from the finals case maps Patient, Appointment, QuestionnaireResponse, Task, Communication, RelatedPerson/consent representation, and Provenance as applicable;
- a pinned validator returns a passing report before the UI says `Validated`;
- the mapping and validator output are inspectable;
- validation failure is visible and blocks the `Validated` label;
- the UI states that a valid FHIR artifact is not an Epic/Ochsner connection or writeback.

### ML — Calibrated LightGBM and SHAP, last

**User outcome:** The staff queue can explain why one treatment encounter was prioritized for supportive outreach without presenting the score as clinical risk.

**Model definition:**

- one trained model only: shallow, regularized `lightgbm.LGBMClassifier`;
- target: `unresolved attendance disruption by the intervention cutoff`;
- output use: supportive outreach ordering only;
- explicit barriers and deterministic cutoffs always override the model;
- deterministic policy, contact-all, and contact-none are evaluation comparators and runtime fallbacks, not trained classifiers.

**Data and split:**

- versioned longitudinal training-data generator with repeated fictional patients, timestamped signals, missingness, rural/urban access, transport status, channel delivery, intervention history, outcomes, site effects, and drift;
- patient-separated and chronological train/calibration/test partitions;
- final test set remains untouched until model/calibrator selection is frozen;
- post-cutoff outcomes, final cancellation reasons, future notes, and actions created after scoring are leakage and must be rejected by tests.

**Calibration:**

- fit LightGBM on the training partition;
- fit a sigmoid calibrator on the distinct calibration partition by default;
- isotonic calibration may be evaluated only when the calibration sample is sufficiently large and it wins on held-out calibration without overfitting;
- persist model, calibrator, feature schema, generator seed, training timestamp, and evaluation manifest as one versioned artifact set.
- export a TypeScript-consumable predictor and calibrator plus a SHAP explanation bound to the exact finals feature-snapshot hash; the deployed Vercel application does not train models.

Scikit-learn recommends calibrating on data independent from model fitting, and warns that isotonic calibration can overfit when calibration samples are much smaller than 1,000: <https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html>.

**SHAP explanations:**

- use `shap.TreeExplainer` on the underlying LightGBM raw margin;
- compute and version the finals SHAP explanation during the offline pipeline, then serve it only when the model, feature schema, and runtime feature-snapshot hashes match;
- show the calibrated probability separately;
- display the top directional contributors with feature values and snapshot timestamps;
- label contributions as model associations, not causes and not additive pieces of the calibrated probability;
- never show a patient-facing message such as `AI thinks you will miss treatment`.

LightGBM supplies `LGBMClassifier` probability output and SHAP supports LightGBM tree ensembles through `TreeExplainer`: <https://lightgbm.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pythonapi/lightgbm.LGBMClassifier.html>, <https://shap.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/shap.TreeExplainer.html>.

**Model acceptance gate:**

- Brier score and calibration plot/intercept/slope;
- PR-AUC;
- precision, recall, and workload at navigator capacity `K`;
- subgroup sample counts and performance across rurality, age band, language/channel, digital access, transport need, and service line;
- comparison with deterministic policy, contact-all, and contact-none;
- reproducible seed and leakage-test pass;
- model is rejected if prespecified calibration/top-K value is not met or subgroup harm is unacceptable.

**Failure behavior:** Missing, malformed, stale, or incompatible artifacts produce `Score unavailable`. Universal readiness cadence and deterministic routing continue. Failure must never appear as low risk.

- Partner Dispatch decline, unavailable provider, cancellation, stale assignment, return pending, and backup activation;
- missed department SLA;
- appointment time change invalidating the old cutoff/ride;
- caregiver permission revoked;
- clinical text excluded from caregiver visual, accessibility, search, export, and outbound content;
- model missing, stale, malformed, or feature-schema mismatch;
- reset/replay with network disabled.

### Finals safeguards

- preflight Twilio/ElevenLabs credentials, webhook reachability, test number, and Partner Dispatch reset state;
- give one concise verbal environment disclosure before the integrated sequence; do not turn it into persistent UI decoration;
- present ordinary product statuses such as `Message delivered`, `Call completed`, `Trip requested`, `Driver assigned`, and `Patient acknowledged` only when backed by the corresponding callback or deterministic replay event;
- keep a deterministic local replay for each network step;
- never silently switch a failed live action to a fake success;
- keep a recorded backup run for the finals presentation;
- rehearse one path and one failure; avoid free-form exploration during the primary pitch.
