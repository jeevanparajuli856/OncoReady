# ML-001 — Longitudinal LightGBM and SHAP prioritization

## User-visible outcome

Staff can see why Camila's treatment encounter was prioritized and why SMS-first outreach followed by voice or human escalation is recommended, without presenting the model as clinical risk or autonomous decision-making.

## Product/demo impact

This slice makes the ML claim real and inspectable: a reproducible longitudinal synthetic dataset, leakage-safe LightGBM training, calibration, held-out evaluation, SHAP evidence, and a non-hard-coded Camila trajectory drive supportive outreach priority.

## In scope

- Versioned generator for at least 75,000 encounters, 8,000 fictional patients, 12 fictional centers, and 18 months with repeated encounters, missingness, site effects, and controlled drift.
- Patient-separated chronological train/calibration/test partitions and explicit leakage/schema tests.
- One shallow regularized `lightgbm.LGBMClassifier`, sigmoid calibration by default, prespecified evaluation gate, baselines, subgroup analysis, and reproducibility evidence.
- One versioned artifact set containing model, calibrator, feature schema, seeds, generator version, thresholds, training time, evaluation manifest, and data/model card.
- SHAP TreeExplainer on the underlying model margin and separate display of calibrated probability.
- Normal-pipeline Camila T−7/T−2/T−1 trajectory, runtime inference, compatibility/staleness checks, and safe degradation.

## Out of scope

- Clinical risk prediction, diagnosis, treatment-access decisions, autonomous channel agents, causal claims, or patient-facing AI judgments.
- Training on Epic Sandbox data or silently blending Epic resources into synthetic outcomes.
- Hard-coded Camila scores, explanations, or channel choices.
- Production clinical validation or a claim that synthetic performance generalizes to patients.

## Architecture impact

- Adds a versioned offline data/training pipeline and bounded API inference component using the `FLOW-001` feature schema.
- Model output influences supportive-outreach ordering only; explicit barriers and deterministic deadlines retain precedence.
- Missing, stale, malformed, or incompatible artifacts return `Score unavailable` while universal deterministic cadence continues.
- `frontend_design_required=true`; trajectory and explanation extend existing data-visualization language.

## Contract impact

Required. Define versioned feature input, inference result, model/artifact metadata, staleness/compatibility errors, calibrated probability, SHAP-margin contributions, and outreach-policy consumption.

## Test depth

FULL. Independent tests cover generator reproducibility, partitions, leakage, schema/artifact compatibility, calibration/evaluation, subgroup gates, Camila-not-hard-coded behavior, runtime failure, and end-to-end policy integration.

## Security risk

STANDARD with dedicated review. Material concerns are artifact integrity/deserialization, training/inference data boundaries, misleading output/claims, resource exhaustion, and unauthorized explanation access.

## Dependencies

- Stable `FLOW-001` event and feature snapshot contract; runtime integration waits for its merged revision.
- Prespecified navigator capacity `K`, calibration/top-K acceptance values, minimum subgroup counts, and rejection rule from [LAUNCH_SCENARIO_SETTINGS.md](../LAUNCH_SCENARIO_SETTINGS.md), frozen before opening the final test partition. Task review may refine them before evaluation, never in response to final-test results.

## Evaluation and downstream integration

Freeze cohort/time boundaries and a patient-level split manifest before training. Every encounter for a patient belongs to only one partition; document exclusion of encounters outside its chronological window. Keep the signature Camila scenario outside model fitting/calibration and final-test tuning. Do not repeatedly inspect the final holdout to choose a model that tells the desired story.

The accepted inference contract/artifact is an upstream dependency of OUTREACH-001. Thresholds and subgroup rejection rules are reviewable planning inputs, not values selected after seeing test results. Model unavailability degrades scoring while API sessions and deterministic workflow remain usable; release preflight still requires an accepted artifact.

## Acceptance criteria

1. The generator reproducibly creates at least 75,000 encounters across at least 8,000 fictional patients, 12 fictional centers, and 18 months with all roadmap-required readiness, engagement, access, outcome, missingness, site, and drift factors.
2. Patient-separated chronological train/calibration/test partitions keep the final test set untouched until model and calibrator freeze.
3. Automated leakage tests reject post-cutoff outcomes, final cancellation reasons, future notes, and actions after the scoring timestamp.
4. LightGBM fits only on training data; sigmoid calibration fits only on the distinct calibration partition; isotonic is used only if prespecified held-out evidence supports it without overfit.
5. The frozen artifact gate reports Brier score, calibration intercept/slope/plot, PR-AUC, precision/recall/workload at `K`, required baselines, subgroup sample counts/performance, and reproducible seeds.
6. Prespecified calibration/top-K or subgroup-harm failure rejects the artifact; it is not shipped with explanatory wording alone.
7. The versioned artifact set contains model, calibrator, schema, generator version/seed, training time, thresholds, evaluation manifest, and a data/model card that states synthetic limitations.
8. Camila's held-out T−7/T−2/T−1 sequence runs through the normal feature/inference pipeline and is proven not to have a hard-coded score, explanation, or channel choice.
9. The staff UI shows calibrated probability separately from dated SHAP-margin associations, labels them as associations rather than causes, and explains SMS-first plus the voice/human escalation condition.
10. Explicit patient response supersedes prediction immediately; missing, stale, malformed, or incompatible artifacts show `Score unavailable`, never low risk, while deterministic cadence continues.
