# UI-001 — Workspace visual continuity and motion polish

## User-visible outcome

OncoReady keeps the recently approved Clinical Glass / Continuity Aurora landing direction, adds restrained one-time card reveals as visitors scroll, and carries the same visual language into every patient, staff, caregiver, system, sign-in, and shared-shell surface.

Scrolling must feel continuous rather than slow or pausy. Motion should help sections arrive and clarify hierarchy, never delay reading, block interaction, or compete with treatment-readiness information. Users who prefer reduced motion, or use OncoReady's motion-off control, receive the same content and state meaning immediately without entrance effects.

## Product and pitch impact

The first impression and the complete Maria journey should now feel like one finished SaaS business solution instead of a polished public page leading into visually older workspaces. The primary treatment-readiness journey remains functionally unchanged, but presentation becomes coherent from the landing page through patient check-in, staff resolution, permission-limited caregiver status, patient confirmation, and the system graph/timeline.

The public landing page must use confident buyer-oriented product, value, and workflow language. It may invite visitors to explore the product and its workspaces, but it must not describe OncoReady or the landing experience as a demo, prototype, preview, portfolio artifact, or training environment. Truthfulness is an internal writing constraint, not a public-facing caveat or disclaimer theme.

The landing section headed **“Built around everyone responsible for tomorrow.”** is the signature scroll-reveal moment: patient, staff, and caregiver cards visibly emerge as they enter view using distinct but related variants. The existing **Deployment paths** section is reframed as a credible SaaS business model that explains who buys OncoReady, what the offering provides, and how an organization can expand it, without invented pricing, customers, deployments, integrations, compliance, or outcomes.

## Current-state diagnosis

Inspection of the current frontend identifies several plausible contributors to the reported scroll hesitation:

- the global page background uses `background-attachment: fixed`, which can force expensive repaints during scroll;
- sticky/full-width chrome and many cards use `backdrop-filter`, increasing compositing cost when large layers move beneath them;
- the landing page combines a long document, a canvas continuity field, imagery, a map, gradients, shadows, and translucent surfaces;
- the staff workspace introduces its own `overflow-y-auto` scroll region, which can feel discontinuous beside the document scroll;
- new section reveals would add more work if implemented with raw scroll listeners, per-card observers that remain active, layout reads, or continuous animation loops.

The existing continuity field already caps device-pixel ratio, pauses when offscreen or hidden, and settles when idle; those safeguards must remain. Implementation should validate the diagnosis in a production build and remove or bound the actual repaint/compositing causes rather than merely changing easing.

- Give the patient, staff, and caregiver role cards distinct but coherent reveal variants; exact motion direction, distance, timing, and easing belong to the frontend design phase.
- Use restrained variation for other selected card groups where it improves pacing; do not animate every element or create continuous ambient page motion.
- Diagnose and improve scroll smoothness across the landing page and workspace surfaces, including the known fixed-background, broad blur/compositing, canvas, map, and nested-scroll candidates.
- Extend established Clinical Glass tokens and component language across:

  - sign-in gateway and workspace/auth modal;

## Out of scope

- Workflow reducer, task transitions, fixture semantics, local-persistence behavior, caregiver permissions, or clinical-routing changes.
- Backend, database, authentication, authorization, API, contract, production integration, analytics, infrastructure, or deployment work.
- A new logo, palette, font system, landing information architecture, route system, or replacement of Clinical Glass / Continuity Aurora.
- Pricing amounts, billing or checkout, sales lead capture, CRM integration, customer logos, testimonials, named deployments, usage claims, outcome claims, compliance claims, or promises of live EHR/vendor connectivity.
- Public-facing prototype/demo disclaimers or limitations copy presented as a substitute for clear product positioning.
- Continuous parallax, scroll-jacking, custom scroll physics, pinned storytelling, autoplay carousels, or several simultaneous ambient effects.
- A general-purpose animation framework or a new motion dependency when existing React, CSS, `motion`, and browser observers are sufficient.
- Broad data or feature changes unrelated to visual continuity, truthful SaaS framing, accessibility, or scroll performance.

## Architecture impact

- **Database:** false
- **Backend:** false
- **Frontend:** true
- **Frontend design required:** true
- **Infrastructure:** false
- Reuse the current React + TypeScript + Vite application, workflow state, route/perspective callbacks, Tailwind/CSS tokens, `motion` dependency, and established design system.
- Treat reveal state as local presentation state. It must not enter the workflow reducer, browser persistence, audit event model, or caregiver projection.
- Prefer one small shared reveal primitive/hook or equivalent coherent pattern using `IntersectionObserver`. It must support one-time reveal, grouped/staggered variants, an immediately visible fallback, and effective reduced motion without exposing a cross-component contract.
- Do not add raw document scroll handlers or a reveal-specific animation-frame loop. Once an item has revealed, stop observing it. Avoid layout reads during animation and restrict animated properties to compositor-friendly opacity/transform where practical.
- Remove, replace, or tightly limit the inspected repaint-heavy scroll effects, especially the fixed body background and broad blur layers in primary scrolling contexts. Keep glass hierarchy through cheaper opaque/translucent fallbacks where needed.
- Preserve the continuity field's capped resolution, offscreen/hidden pause, idle settling, static fallback, and reduced-motion behavior. Scroll polish must not turn it into a continuous render loop.
- The staff shell may retain a contained scroll region only if it remains deliberate, has no nested-scroll trap, and wheel/touch/keyboard scrolling stays continuous; otherwise use the document scroll.
- No ADR or system-architecture change is required because this is a bounded presentation/performance refinement inside the existing frontend.

## Risks and mitigations

- **Additional reveal motion could worsen the reported scroll pauses.** Use one shared observer strategy, reveal once, unobserve completed elements, avoid scroll listeners/per-frame layout reads, and validate the production build at mobile and presentation-laptop sizes.
- **Fixed backgrounds, large blur regions, shadows, canvas, or map compositing could continue to repaint during scroll.** Audit with browser rendering/performance tools, remove or bound confirmed offenders, pause offscreen work, and prefer cheaper static/translucent fallbacks while preserving hierarchy.
- **Hidden-before-reveal content could disappear when observers or scripts fail.** Content is visible by default; progressive enhancement may opt into a pre-reveal state only after support is established. Interaction and assistive-technology access cannot wait for animation.
- **Motion could distract, delay task completion, or cause discomfort.** Entrances are short, low-amplitude, one-time, non-blocking, and absent under effective reduced motion. Operational actions and urgent state changes do not wait for decorative motion.
- **Applying the public theme everywhere could reduce staff scan density or obscure clinical status.** Reuse tokens and surfaces, not marketing scale; preserve dense staff layout, clear hierarchy, labeled status, deadlines, owners, focus states, and non-color cues.
- **A SaaS business-model section could imply offerings or traction that do not exist.** Describe the intended institutional buyer, product capability, configuration, and expansion logic without pricing, customers, contractual promises, compliance, production deployment, integrations, or measured outcomes; do not turn those internal constraints into landing-page disclaimers.
- **Broad visual edits could break the deterministic journey or caregiver boundary.** Keep workflow/state code authoritative and run the complete Maria journey, reset, route reachability, and caregiver clinical-data exclusion after the refresh.

## Acceptance criteria

### Landing direction, positioning, and scroll motion

- The approved LAND-001 hero, official identity, Continuity Aurora mood, content order, navigation anchors, workspace entry, readiness proof, map, FAQ, and closing CTA remain recognizable and functional; UI-001 does not replace the landing design.
- Public landing copy presents OncoReady as a finished SaaS treatment-readiness business solution and explains product value and workflow in confident buyer-oriented language.
- Landing-visible labels, badges, section copy, and CTAs do not call OncoReady or the experience a demo, prototype, portfolio project, training environment, or preview. Visitors may be invited to “explore the product” or “explore workspaces,” but not to enter a demo/training experience.
- The landing does not add caveat/disclaimer sections about honesty, limitations, or prototype status. It remains truthful by avoiding unsupported customer, pricing, deployment, integration, compliance, scale, validation, and outcome claims.
- When the “Built around everyone responsible for tomorrow.” role-card group first enters the viewport, all three patient/staff/caregiver cards visibly reveal with distinct but coherent variants. The variants are not identical copies, yet read as one motion family and preserve the intended card order.
- Selected later card groups may use related reveal variants to create rhythm, but the page does not animate every child or run constant decorative motion.
- Each entrance runs at most once per mounted landing-page session, does not capture scrolling, does not delay pointer/keyboard interaction, does not shift layout, and leaves content fully visible after completion.
- Reveal enhancement uses observer-driven threshold changes rather than raw scroll listeners or a reveal-specific animation-frame loop; completed elements are unobserved and animation performs no repeated layout measurement.
- If `IntersectionObserver`, animation support, or the enhancement fails, content is visible and usable without waiting for a trigger.

### Scroll performance and reduced motion

- The fixed body background and broad scroll-attached blur/filter layers identified in the current implementation are audited and removed, replaced, or constrained so they do not repaint large primary scrolling surfaces unnecessarily.
- The existing continuity field remains capped, idle-settling, offscreen/hidden-pausing, and independent of document scrolling. Selected reveal components create no new continuous render loop.
- In a production build, continuous wheel/trackpad scrolling through the full landing page at a 1440×900 presentation viewport and touch-style scrolling at a 390×844 viewport show no obvious repeatable pause at section boundaries, role-card reveals, the map, or the SaaS section. Browser inspection shows no reveal-caused long task of 50 ms or more during the checked pass.
- Patient, caregiver, system, sign-in, and staff surfaces scroll without horizontal overflow or nested-scroll traps at supported sizes. Keyboard Page Up/Page Down and touch/wheel input move the expected primary scroll container.
- When either `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` or OncoReady's motion-off control is active, cards and sections render immediately in their final positions, smooth scrolling becomes immediate, the continuity field is static, and all content, focus, status, and route behavior remain equivalent.

### Workspace visual continuity

- Public, sign-in, patient, staff, caregiver, system, modal, header/footer, and mobile workspace-dock surfaces use the established Clinical Glass color/type/surface/control vocabulary rather than the older hard-outlined sticker treatment.
- The patient home, readiness modal, in-progress/ready states, and patient resolution view preserve one-clear-action hierarchy, treatment details, validation, disabled/success feedback, and current functional actions.
- The staff shell and all eight staff destinations—Command Center, Exceptions, Patients, case workspace, Resources, Insights, Integrations, and Admin—share the theme while retaining compact information density, legible ownership/deadline/status cues, and fast navigation.
- The caregiver view preserves its deliberately narrow transportation-only projection and the explicit privacy boundary; no clinical concern text or nurse-only detail becomes visible, searchable, or accessible there.
- The system view keeps the Treatment Readiness Graph, audit timeline, deterministic architecture explanation, filters, and status transitions legible. Motion and color are never the only carriers of graph or event state.
- The sign-in gateway and auth/workspace selector remain truthful local role entry and every role entry reaches the same existing perspective as before; public landing copy does not use internal synthetic/training framing to cheapen the product.
- Hover, focus, pressed, selected, disabled, empty/no-result, validation, in-progress, and success states remain visually clear where applicable. Focus is visible, semantic control names remain intact, and primary public/patient targets remain at least 44px.

### SaaS business model and truthful presentation

- The current “Deployment paths” area is visibly reframed as OncoReady's SaaS business model rather than only three technical deployment shapes.
- The section clearly explains: the institutional buyer/customer type; the treatment-readiness capability being subscribed to; how configuration/implementation supports a center or health system; and how the model can expand across sites or roles.
- Cards and CTA copy present a credible commercial structure without inventing price points, billing mechanics, customers, contracts, production availability, live integrations, compliance/certification, clinical validation, scale, or measured outcomes. Future capabilities are framed positively but not presented as currently live.
- The SaaS section remains consistent with OncoReady's real current product behavior and routes its CTA to an existing product/workspace entry rather than a fake checkout or sales flow.

### Regression, responsiveness, and primary journey

- At 320px mobile, 768px tablet, 1440×900 presentation laptop, and a wide desktop viewport, intended surfaces remain readable, controls do not clip, cards do not cause horizontal page overflow, and sticky/fixed chrome does not cover focused content.
- The primary journey still passes: landing scroll and SaaS story → workspace entry → patient readiness submission → two owned staff tasks → clinical acknowledgment → transportation confirmation → caregiver transportation-only update → patient acknowledgment → confirmed graph/timeline → reset.
- Existing workflow transitions, refresh/persistence behavior, search/filter behavior, workspace navigation, FAQ, map fallback, role switching, reset, and required synthetic/simulated disclosures inside the product workflow remain functional.
- The production build, existing critical-path smoke suite, focused reveal/reduced-motion/copy checks, and independent targeted test report pass on the integrated revision.
